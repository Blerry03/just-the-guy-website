Let's Talk Gutters.

Straightforward answers to some of the questions homeowners ask most often.

GUTTER MAINTENANCE

How Often Should You Clean Your Gutters?

For many homes, checking and cleaning gutters at least twice a year is a reasonable starting point—often in the spring and fall.

Homes surrounded by trees may need attention more often, especially during heavy leaf and seed drop.

  • Watch for water spilling over gutter edges.
  • Look for plants or debris growing inside gutters.
  • Check for staining or water collecting near your foundation.
  • Pay attention to downspouts that aren't draining properly.
Our tip: Avoid climbing onto your roof or using an unstable ladder to inspect gutters. Professional cleaning is the safer choice when access is difficult.
GUTTER PROTECTION

Are Gutter Guards Worth It?

Gutter guards can help reduce the amount of leaves and larger debris entering your gutters, which may make routine maintenance easier.

However, no gutter guard completely eliminates maintenance. Small debris can still accumulate, and guards may occasionally need to be inspected or cleaned.

  • They can help reduce larger debris buildup.
  • Different styles work better in different conditions.
  • They should be installed with proper water flow in mind.
  • Periodic inspections are still important.
Our tip: The right guard depends on your roof, surrounding trees, and the type of debris your home receives.
WATER MANAGEMENT

Where Should Your Downspouts Drain?

Gutters collect water from your roof, but downspouts determine where that water ends up.

Ideally, water should be directed away from the foundation to a suitable drainage location. The right solution depends on your property's slope, soil, landscaping, and drainage setup.

  • Watch for pooling near the foundation.
  • Make sure discharge points aren't blocked.
  • Check that extensions remain connected and positioned correctly.
  • Keep drainage directed away from neighboring properties.
Our tip: If water repeatedly collects around your foundation, the gutter system and property drainage should both be evaluated.
WHEN TO CALL FOR HELP

Signs Your Gutters Need Attention.

You don't have to wait until a gutter falls off the house to recognize that something isn't right.

Many gutter problems start small and become more noticeable during heavy rain.

  • Water overflowing from the gutters.
  • Sections pulling away from the fascia.
  • Visible leaks at corners or joints.
  • Standing water that doesn't drain after rainfall.
  • Downspouts that are loose, crushed, or disconnected.
Our tip: Some problems can be corrected with cleaning, resealing, or adjustments rather than replacing the entire system.
SEAMLESS GUTTERS

What Makes Seamless Gutters Different?

Seamless gutters are formed to fit the measurements of your home, reducing the number of seams along straight gutter runs.

Fewer seams can mean fewer potential leak points compared with sectional gutter systems. Corners, end caps, and outlets still require proper installation and maintenance.

  • Custom-formed for the building.
  • Clean, continuous appearance.
  • Fewer joints along straight runs.
  • Available in different sizes and colors.

YEAR-ROUND HOME CARE

Michigan Weather Doesn't Take a Break.

Every season brings different challenges. Here are a few things to keep in mind throughout the year.

Spring

After the Thaw.

Look for winter damage, loose gutters, blocked downspouts, and drainage problems as snow melts and spring rain arrives.

Summer

Storm Season.

Check for storm debris and watch how your gutters handle heavy rain. Overflowing water may point to a blockage or drainage issue.

Fall

Leaf Season.

Falling leaves and debris can quickly fill gutters. Clearing them before freezing temperatures arrive can help reduce winter problems.

Winter

Freezing Weather.

Watch for unusual ice buildup and drainage problems. Ice dams can involve insulation, ventilation, snow, and roof conditions, not just the gutters.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Questions? We've Got Answers.

A few quick answers to help you understand your gutter system.

Can overflowing gutters damage my home?

Yes. Repeated overflow can contribute to problems with fascia, siding, landscaping, and drainage near the foundation. The cause should be identified and corrected.

Do gutter guards mean I never have to clean my gutters?

No. Gutter guards can reduce debris entering the system, but periodic inspection and maintenance are still recommended.

Can a leaking gutter be repaired instead of replaced?

Often, yes. Some leaks can be addressed with cleaning, resealing, or adjustments. Severe damage or deterioration may require replacement.

How do I know if I need larger gutters?

Gutter sizing depends on factors such as roof area, pitch, rainfall, and downspout capacity. An evaluation can help determine whether your current system is appropriately sized.

No. We're based in Durand but serve communities throughout Mid-Michigan. Availability can vary depending on your location and project.
